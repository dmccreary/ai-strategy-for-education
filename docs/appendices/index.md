# Appendices

Supplementary material that complements the main chapters of *AI Strategy for
Education*. These appendices are written to stand on their own — shared with a
colleague, handed to a student, or dropped into a workshop — without requiring the
full course.

## The MicroStrategies Series

Three companion collections of small, low-risk, high-leverage ideas for putting AI
to work in a school. Each is written for a different audience, and together they
span the whole institution — from the boardroom, to the classroom, to the student's
own desk.

- **[MicroStrategies for Leaders](microstrategies.md)** — Thirty *organizational*,
  top-down strategies for getting a school, district, college, or university to
  adopt AI: setting a vision, securing sponsorship, building a coalition, lowering
  barriers, earning trust through policy and privacy, proving value with pilots, and
  making the change stick. Best for administrators, committees, and change leaders.

- **[MicroStrategies for Teachers](microstrategies-adopting-ai.md)** — Twenty small,
  bottom-up mini-projects a single teacher can try in a week, with no new budget.
  They begin with quiet teacher-productivity wins, move into the classroom with a
  first MicroSim, and grow into shared department practice. Best for an individual
  teacher looking for a safe first step.

- **[MicroStrategies for Students](microstrategies-students.md)** — Thirty
  student-facing ideas for using generative AI as a partner for *learning*: getting
  started safely, using AI as a personal tutor, thinking critically about its
  answers, creating and exploring, and building habits that last. Written to be
  shared directly with students.

## Where to Start

The three guides are designed to reinforce one another. A common path is **top-down
meets bottom-up**: leaders use their guide to clear the runway — policy, access, and
permission — while teachers use theirs to rack up the small, visible wins that prove
AI is worth the effort. The students' guide then makes sure the learners themselves
use these tools to *think more*, not less. Start with whichever audience you belong
to, and pass the other two along.
